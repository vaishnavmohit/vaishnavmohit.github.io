For most of my career, I optimized for a different metric than the one that matters in a startup. In research, you're rewarded for being *right* — for a result that holds up under scrutiny. In a company, you're rewarded for being *useful* — for something a customer will rely on tomorrow morning.

Kimova AI lives at that intersection. We're automating ISO 27001 compliance, and compliance is unforgiving: it has to be correct *and* trustworthy *and* fast. That's exactly the kind of problem my PhD prepared me for without my realizing it.

## What visual reasoning taught me

My doctoral work at Brown University and ANITI looked at how attention and memory let a system reason about what it sees. The recurring lesson: raw capability isn't enough — a model has to know *what to attend to* and *what to ignore*. That's true of compliance automation too. The hard part isn't reading a policy; it's knowing which clause matters for this control, in this context, for this organization.

## The builder's bias

Research rewards patience. Startups reward shipping. The reconciliation, for me, is this: ship something real, then let evidence — not opinion — tell you what to fix next. It's the same loop as a good experiment, just with customers instead of benchmarks.
